Contributor · new book · upcoming

Kirk Watson

New book. The Morrisites: Writings and Revelations (2026), edited by Kirk Watson, collects writings of Joseph Morris, George Williams, William W. Davies, George S. Dove and James Dove, with his introduction. It is the most convenient way to read the movement’s own writings beyond The Spirit Prevails, and buying a copy supports this kind of independent work. Buy it on Amazon (paperback and Kindle).

Upcoming, November 14, 2026, 10:00 a.m. Mountain. “Episode 113: Kirk Watson on Reincarnation in Early Mormonism,” on Robert Boylan’s Scriptural Mormonism podcast, premieres live on YouTube; the conversation includes the Morrisites. Watch on YouTube

New find. Kirk found two revelations of Morris, dated January 14 and 16, 1860, filed among anonymous letters in Brigham Young’s office papers, where a clerk docketed one “Balderdash.” He identified the hand as Morris’s and shared his transcription. They are early forms of revelations later printed in The Spirit Prevails. See Documents.

From the families

How a descendant filled in the Eliason story

Keith Chappelow, a descendant of Erik and Karin Eliasson, wrote to the editor with what his family knew. He sent the family’s account of the Eliasons at Soda Springs and Racetrack, pages from Joseph Smith III and the Restoration (1952), a family group sheet, and pages from a 1989 history of Powell County, Montana.

That led to Family Tree links for Erik and Karin; their 1870 baptisms into the RLDS Church; their daughter Anna, whom an early county history calls the first white child born at Soda Springs; Erik’s brother Elias, who came to Soda Springs in 1863 but is not on the roll; and Joseph Smith III’s 1889 visit to the old camp at Weber with John Parson. See the pages on Erik and Karin.

Is there a Morrisite in your family? Family accounts, letters, photographs, group sheets and local histories can answer questions no archive can. Share what you have; nothing is published without your permission.

From the community · local history groups

What local history groups added

In November the editor shared this guide in local history groups on Facebook in places where the Morrisites settled, beginning with Deer Lodge, Montana, and Walla Walla, Washington. Within a day the replies were changing the guide.

Deer Lodge. A reader who once worked for a Soren Beck near Avon pointed the way to a 1902 biography of Soren R. Beck, who came to work on Niels R. Beck’s ranch in 1884 and was probably his nephew. A great-grandson of George Johnson, the last official leader of the Montana Morrisites, who preached at the Lord’s House near Racetrack until 1954, joined the conversation. So did descendants of families who built the church and worshiped there, neighbors who want to see it preserved, and a volunteer at the Deer Lodge County museum, which holds a pump organ said to have come from the Morrisites.

Walla Walla. A reader asked whether the editor had checked Whitman College and recalled that the court records of the lawsuit against William W. Davies had gone missing from the county courthouse. Following up led to the case file in the Washington State Archives and to Whitman’s copies of depositions and an 1882 photograph of the Davies family and home. See Repositories.

Thank you to everyone who has commented, shared and written. If your family or town has a Morrisite story, a photograph or an old record, share what you have. Nothing is published without your permission, and people are named only if they wish.

Added to this guide

  1. 5 Nov 2026The man who went for help. Lars Christian Geertsen, one of the men the Morrisites held in their log-house jail in 1862, escaped and carried word to Salt Lake City. A Church History Department profile now confirms that he is the same man who settled at Salmon, Idaho, where he ranched at Geertson Creek and built a log house, later listed on the National Register of Historic Places, whose upstairs dance hall served the community into the 1920s. Readers in Salmon pointed the way. See Lars Christian Geertsen.
  2. 5 Nov 2026A 1982 newspaper story. The Montana Standard of June 19, 1982, ran “Deer Lodge Once Thought to Be Site of Second Coming,” with photographs of the Racetrack church; Kris Pozega, a great-great-niece of George Johnson, brought it to light. See the Bibliography.
  3. 4 Nov 2026Where did the reincarnation teaching come from? With help from Kirk Watson, the question now sets out C. LeRoy Anderson’s theory that Morris took the idea from Charles B. Thompson, and the case against it: the idea was already current among Latter-day Saints in the 1850s, and Morris dated it to a revelation of 1859. It also adds Israel A. J. Dennis, who founded a reincarnationist “Church of the First Born” with no known Morrisite tie, and corrects the chronology on James Brighouse, whose ideas have not been traced to the Morrisites. See Questions and History.
  4. 4 Nov 2026What became of them. The later paths of the people on the roll now have their own section on Who they were, beside the paths that brought them into the movement.
  5. 4 Nov 2026The 1880 census. One census page at Walla Walla shows William W. Davies’s Kingdom of Heaven in the summer it began to break up: Davies, a widower, with Sven Hagg in his house and David Park, Ole Hagg, the Sorensons and others around him, months after the deaths of his sons Arthur and David. The same census finds Erik Eliasson farming in the Deer Lodge Valley while his family stayed at Soda Springs, and settles who John Garrett’s wife was. See William W. Davies and Erik Eliasson.
  6. 4 Nov 2026The “Walla Walla Jesus” case. The 1880 lawsuit against Davies, reported missing from the county courthouse, survives in the Washington State Archives as a territorial court case file, and Whitman College holds copies of depositions and an 1882 photograph of the Davies family and home. See Repositories.
  7. 4 Nov 2026Land records. Federal land patents traced for about twenty roll members and their families, from David Park’s claim in the Carson Valley in 1864 to Walla Walla in 1890, with the Deer Lodge Valley, Soda Springs and Blackfoot between. See the notes on the roll and Repositories.
  8. 4 Nov 2026Three new pages. Niels R. Beck, John Garrett and David Park bring the pages on people of the roll to thirty-seven.
  9. 3 Nov 2026The Eliasons and the Christoffersens. A family group sheet and a 1989 Powell County history add Erik Eliasson’s brother Elias, the family’s move to the Deer Lodge Valley, and the Christoffersens’ road north to Montana. See Erik Eliasson.
  10. 3 Nov 2026The prisoners and the trials. The Deseret News named the ninety-eight men brought before Judge Kinney after the siege in June 1862, and the seven convicted of murder in March 1863, with their sentences. The posse lost two men, not one. See Documents and Questions.
  11. 3 Nov 2026Paths into the movement. Who they were now asks what brought Latter-day Saints to Morris: those who reached Utah in 1859–60 joined at about twice the rate of earlier emigrants. The roll now has 150 names linked to FamilySearch, 30 family groups, and notes on 92 people.
  12. 3 Nov 2026Pages on people of the roll. Thirty-four members now have pages of their own, with what the sources say about them and full citations; look for the page tag on the roll.
  13. Nov 2026Documents and the posse order. A new page gathers the primary documents that can be read online, including Acting Governor Fuller’s order of June 11, 1862, calling out the militia. See Documents.
  14. Nov 2026Who they were. The people on the roll counted by sex, language, age, birthplace, arrival in Utah and where they died, with 91 of them read in the 1860 census. See Who they were.
  15. Nov 2026The Racetrack church in photographs. Twenty photographs taken September 5, 1989, courtesy of Barbara B. Walden, Community of Christ Historic Sites Foundation, open a dated record of the church to 2018, with views of South Weber and the cannonballs. See Images.
  16. Nov 2026The roll of membership. The 1861–62 roll, printed in 1886: 430 names, checked against the pamphlet and searchable. See the Roll of membership.
