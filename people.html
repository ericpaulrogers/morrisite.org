Who they were

The people on the roll

Who joined Joseph Morris? This page counts what can be said of the 430 people on the 1861–62 roll of membership. Some figures cover everyone on the roll, judged from their names. The rest come from the people identified so far in FamilySearch Family Tree, and hold only for them.

The roll is not a full list of members. Its compiler wrote that “many persons baptized” were never recorded. The lists behind these figures can be downloaded, and counted in other ways, on the Data page. Corrections and new identifications are welcome at contact@morrisite.org.

91 people on the roll have been found in the United States census of Utah Territory, taken June–September 1860, the year before Morris began baptizing at South Weber (80 of them among those linked to Family Tree). The census gives each adult’s occupation and the value of the real estate and personal property they owned. Figures were read from the page images on FamilySearch; the index there does not include them. People who reached Utah after the census, such as the Forscutts and the Swiss families, are not in it.

Paths into the movement

What brought Latter-day Saints to Joseph Morris? The members left few accounts of their reasons, so this section tests what the records can show: when people reached Utah, whom they crossed the plains with, where they came from, and whether they joined as families. Companies are known for 98 of the 178 people linked to Family Tree, from the Mormon Pioneer Overland Travel records attached there and from the rosters of the Church History Biographical Database (CHD). These are associations in the records, not reasons the members gave.

Roll members per 1,000 people in the CHD’s pioneer companies of each year. The highest rate is 1859 (18 of 1,606). People who had arrived in 1859 and 1860, a year or two before Morris began baptizing, joined about twice as often as those who came in 1853–56, and far more often than those who came before 1853. Those who arrived in 1861 had little time to join before the roll closed. CHD counts are the names it holds, not every emigrant; the roll members identified so far are not a random sample, and some were found by searching these same rosters.

Weighing the factors

FactorWhat the records showHow far it goes
Recent arrival63 of 117 identified people with a year of arrival reached Utah in 1856 or later; the rate per 1,000 emigrants peaks in 1859.Strong pattern, the clearest in the records. Why newcomers joined more often (fewer ties to their wards, less property, hardship on arrival) is not yet measured.
Crossing together65 of 98 with a known company shared it with another roll family.Moderate. It overlaps with origin and with how the names were found.
Language and origin168 of 430 names on the roll are Scandinavian.Strong for the roll as a whole, but it overlaps with arrival year; how it compares with the population of Utah in 1861 has not been measured.
Family127 roll names belong to the 30 family groups found so far (see the roll).Strong where known: families often joined together, so one household’s decision accounts for several names. Many relationships on the roll are still unfound.
Place57 of the 91 roll members found in the 1860 census lived in Davis or Weber County, near South Weber.Strong but expected: Morris preached and baptized there.
Leaders and wardsThe South Weber bishop, Richard Cook, and his brother John joined; sixteen were cut off together on February 11, 1861.Not counted. Followed through the roll notes and person pages.

The factors overlap: a Danish family who crossed in 1859 and settled near South Weber counts under four of them at once, so they cannot be added up. A fair reading is that Morris drew most on recent emigrants, Danish and British, who came as families and lived near him, and least on Saints settled since the early 1850s.

People whom the sources name as Morrisites, or as followers of a later offshoot, but who are not on the printed roll of 1861–62. With Morris, 1861–62 (6)

Named as Morrisites in 1861–62 but not on the printed roll.

  • Morris, Joseph (died June 15, 1862) Morrisite
    The prophet himself. The printed roll lists his followers, not Morris. His wife Mary Olsen, a Danish convert he married soon after a revelation of August 23, 1861, is probably the roll’s “Mary Morris.” The roll’s “Karen Morris” is not identified. In 1878 James Bowman, at Soda Springs, mentions a debt of “Joseph Morris’ Father in Law,” apparently living nearby; he is not named (Church History Library, MS 31876, Letters to Andrew Hendrickson, 1872–1878). His elder brother George’s reminiscences give his life before Utah, his arrival in the fall of 1853 and his earlier marriages, to Mary Thorp, Elizabeth Mills and “Elize Jones” (see Documents). (Anderson, Revisited, 87, 104–5; The Spirit Prevails, 149–51; George Morris reminiscences, CHL MS 1614, 115–22)
  • Bowman, Isabella (died June 15, 1862) Morrisite
    Killed at Kington Fort during the surrender, one of the two women shot by Robert T. Burton’s party. Burton was indicted for her killing in 1870 (the indictment was voided in 1872) and again in 1877, tried from February 18, 1879, and acquitted. Alexander Dow’s affidavit of April 1863 calls her “Mrs. Bowman, wife of James Bowman.” The 1891 Salt Lake Tribune letter says she was holding Joseph March’s baby, whom she had taken in after its mother was killed. Wife of James Bowman (below), who was still a believer at Soda Springs in 1878. Not on the printed roll; she may have joined after it was made. (Salt Lake Herald and Salt Lake Weekly Tribune, Feb.–Mar. 1879 (Burton trial); Salt Lake Tribune, July 26, 1891; Church History Biographical Database (Isabella Bowman); Anderson, Revisited, 148–49)
  • Søndermark, Anders (born 1821) Morrisite
    Born at Vrensted, Jylland, Denmark, January 9, 1821; entered in the draft register as Anders Sørensen, an Aaronic priest. Baptized at South Weber on July 31, 1861, “by Morrisen og Mikkelsen” (by Morris and Mikkelsen), and confirmed by Joseph Morris; a member of the Omaha congregation from 1869, and probably the “Bro. Søndermark” of the Fremont council of November 30, 1871, who said he believed “J. Morris was a Prophet.” Excluded from the Omaha congregation on November 30, 1871. Not on the printed roll. (“Record of members, 1869–1872,” and its draft, “Record of members, circa 1869–1871” (Omaha), MS 32226, Church History Library; translation from the Danish generated by Claude; Fremont council minutes, November 30, 1871, MS 31876)
  • Søndermark, Karen Sørensen (1861–71) Morrisite
    Born at Vrensted, Jylland, Denmark, October 9, 1822; entered in the draft register as Karen Maria Sørensen; wife of Anders Søndermark. Baptized at South Weber on July 31, 1861, “by Morrisen og Mikkelsen,” and confirmed by Joseph Morris; a member of the Omaha congregation from 1869; excluded on November 30, 1871. Not on the printed roll. (“Record of members, 1869–1872,” and its draft, “Record of members, circa 1869–1871” (Omaha), MS 32226, Church History Library; translation from the Danish generated by Claude)
  • Knudsen, Hans (1861–71) Morrisite
    Born on Fyn, Denmark. Baptized at South Weber on July 18, 1861, by Jens Mikkelsen and confirmed by Joseph Morris; a leading member of the Council Bluffs congregation from 1869, baptizing and confirming others there and at Omaha and Crescent City. Not on the printed roll. (“Record of members, circa 1869–1871” (Council Bluffs), MS 32226, Church History Library; translation from the Danish generated by Claude)
  • Fellows, Carleton (born 1809) Morrisite
    Born at Buffalo, New York, August 15, 1809. Baptized at Omaha in 1869 by S. P. Guhl and Hans Knudsen; husband of “Maria Fellows, formerly Maria Guhl,” who had been baptized at South Weber. In 1869 Williams told him he had been Jonas, the father of Simon Peter. Both were excluded from the Council Bluffs congregation on February 5, 1871. (“Record of members, circa 1869–1871” (Council Bluffs), MS 32226, Church History Library; translation from the Danish generated by Claude; record book, 1869, MS 31876)

  • Bowman, James (1862–79) Kington Fort
    Husband of Isabella Bowman, who was killed at the surrender. Held to bail in June 1862; fined $100 for resisting an officer in March 1863; moved to Idaho in the spring of 1863. In 1879 he testified at Burton’s trial that he had lived at Soda Springs since 1863. In August 1878 he wrote from Soda Springs to Erick Eliason in Montana, signing himself “yours truly in the New Covenant”; he had received “the Epistle so kindly sent me by the friends in Deer Lodge” and passed it on to “Father Christofferson,” and among debts owed him he lists one of “Joseph Morris’ Father in Law” (Church History Library, MS 31876, Letters to Andrew Hendrickson, 1872–1878). Not the John Bowman of the roll, who was at Kaysville in 1870 and South Weber in 1880; Anderson also treats them as two men. (Deseret News, June 25, 1862, and April 1, 1863; Salt Lake Herald, Feb. 21, 1879; Anderson, Revisited, 148, 162; Church History Biographical Database (Isabella Bowman))

The Montana church (6)

Leaders of the Church of Jesus Christ of Saints of the Most High in the Deer Lodge Valley, 1870s–1969, who are not on the roll.

  • Williams, George (died 1882) Montana church
    The “Prophet Cainan,” who claimed to be Morris’s successor and led the church that regathered in the Deer Lodge Valley in the 1870s; in January 1879 he wrote that the Montana Saints lived on “the Sides of the North,” where “the City of the great King” would come down before the Lord’s coming (see the Chronology). (Anderson, Revisited; Watson)
  • Hendrikson, Andrew (died July 3, 1921) Montana church
    A Danish immigrant who was never a Latter-day Saint, born at Ondrup, Jutland, on July 29, 1843, and baptized into the Morrisite church in the Missouri River at Omaha on January 22, 1869 (“Record of members, circa 1869–1871,” Council Bluffs, MS 32226; translation from the Danish generated by Claude). In October 1869 Williams named him “the Keeper of the Book” for the Omaha and Council Bluffs fund of free-will offerings (Williams to the Omaha church, October 13, 1869, Church History Library, MS 31876, Letters from Cainan to Andrew Henrikson, 1869). Counselor to William James from May 1882; presided over the Montana church from 1886 until his death. His records, kept by his family, are a main source for Anderson’s book. (Anderson, Revisited)
  • Johnson, George (born in Denmark, 1856; died April 29, 1954) Montana church
    Hendrikson’s associate of more than fifty years, likewise never a Latter-day Saint; presided at the Lord’s House near Racetrack from 1921 until shortly before his death at ninety-eight, the last official leader of the Montana church. A county biography of about 1902 says he came to Montana in the summer of 1873, worked in Deer Lodge hotels, and then took up a stock ranch eight miles south of the town, and that “Johnson and his family are members of the Morrisite church.” On July 1, 1876, he married Josephine V. Oleson, a daughter of Ole Olsen of the 1861–62 roll. (Anderson, Revisited; Progressive Men of the State of Montana (Chicago: A. W. Bowen, [c. 1902]), pp. 1092–93)
  • Beck, Lars P. (1844–1925) Montana church
    Lars Peter Beck. Born in Jylland (Jutland), Denmark; came to America in 1864 and married Maria Christensen at Perth Amboy, New Jersey, on March 7, 1869. Baptized at Fremont, Nebraska, on August 20, 1871, by Andrew Henriksen and Niels Jensen, with his wife Maria; their eldest daughter had been born at Fremont in 1870. Ordained a Seventy in Montana on November 10, 1872, and a member of the council of the Danish-speaking stake from 1873 to 1883; the council met in his house near Deer Lodge on February 21, 1875, and their daughter Margaret was born at Deer Lodge that May. With his brother Nels J. Beck he met William P. Thomas at the Deer Lodge courthouse in 1880. In February 1883 he said he could not see that choosing a president would do the church any good, and he was among the minority who would not accept William James’s election that April; his brother Nels voted for James. On May 26, 1883, the Race Track council released him as a teacher and, with his wife, from membership, saying that the dissenters “had separated themselves from us” (minutes, MS 31876, Minutes, 1883–1884; translation from the Danish generated by Claude). Their children included Annie, Mary Ann, Margaret, Laura, Carl R., Harry H. and Joseph. He died at Deer Lodge on February 21, 1925, and is buried at Hillcrest Cemetery there. (“Record of members, 1869–1872” (Omaha), and minutebooks, 1872–1874, p. 3, and 1881–1890, pp. 10–11, MS 32226, Church History Library; council minutes, 1873–1883, MS 31876, Church History Library (translations from the Danish generated by Claude); Find a Grave 67303646; FamilySearch Family Tree, LK44-Y7K; family tree compiled by descendants of Lars and Maria Beck, shared by the Richardson family (2026))
  • Beck, Maria (1847–1938) Montana church
    Born in Slesvig (Schleswig), on January 7, 1847. Married Lars P. Beck (above) at Perth Amboy, New Jersey, in 1869, and was baptized with him at Fremont, Nebraska, on August 20, 1871, by Niels Jensen and Andrew Henriksen; the family settled near Deer Lodge, Montana, by 1875. Her maiden name was Christensen (Christiansen on her Find a Grave memorial). She died on May 13, 1938, and is buried at Hillcrest Cemetery, Deer Lodge. (“Record of members, 1869–1872” (Omaha), MS 32226, Church History Library (translation from the Danish generated by Claude); Find a Grave 67303647; FamilySearch Family Tree, L4QB-7GT; family tree compiled by descendants of Lars and Maria Beck, shared by the Richardson family (2026))
  • Beck, Nels J. (1847–1921) Montana church
    Nels Jensen Beck, brother of Lars P. Beck (above). Born at Jernved, near Ribe, Denmark, on May 6, 1847; came to America in 1872 and married Marie Helen Hansen at Deer Lodge that year; lived on Cottonwood Creek. The Montana council minutes of May 29, 1880, name “Lars Beck” and “his brother Niels Beck.” At the general assembly of April 25, 1883, “Nels Beck” and his wife voted to sustain William James, while Lars voted against. On April 25, 1887, “N. J. Beck” was made one of the three trustees of the Lord’s House, with George Johnson and Emmanuel Strome, so that the building would not be taxed. Other “Niels Beck” entries in the minutes may be he or the roll’s Niels R. Beck. He died on October 4, 1921, and is buried at Hillcrest Cemetery, Deer Lodge. (Council minutes, May 29, 1880, MS 31876, and elections and court, 1886–87, MS 31876, Church History Library; minutebook, 1881–1890, pp. 10–11, MS 32226, Church History Library; FamilySearch Family Tree, GB7W-C7D; family tree compiled by descendants of Lars and Maria Beck, shared by the Richardson family (2026))

Followers of William W. Davies (24)

Members, missionaries and donors of Davies’s Kingdom of Heaven near Walla Walla, 1867–81, as named in the 1880–81 lawsuit against him and the 1880 census. Many were converts made by his missionaries in San Francisco and Portland who had never been Morrisites; one former Morrisite testified that the Davies doctrine was “not like the Morrisite religion.” Those known or said to have been with the Morrisites before are also tagged Earlier a Morrisite. Davies testified that everyone on his list of donations except Niels R. Beck was a member.

  • Andrews Follower of Davies
    Foreman and agent of the community; Davies sent him east. A former member called him the “destroying angel”; another said he left of his own accord. Given name not recorded. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81)
  • Bowman, Joseph Follower of Davies (probable)
    Called “the first born in the kingdom”; in Davies’s teaching he was counted among Davies’s sons, as Elias reincarnated. Compare Lewis H. Bowman, to whom Davies gave notes in 1879. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81)
  • Cilley, Asa B. (at the camp 1871–79) Follower of Davies
    A blacksmith from Stockton, California, who learned the doctrine at Portland, Oregon, in the winter of 1870–71 and came to the camp in November 1871. He left in June 1879 and sued Davies with Evans and Courtney; the court awarded him $125. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81)
  • Clore, Jesse Y. (born about 1823) Follower of Davies
    Born in Kentucky; first heard the doctrine at Gold Hill, Nevada, and took the vow at San Francisco about 1869. He lived at the camp, homesteaded four miles away, and testified for Davies in 1881 (“Jesse Y. Close” in the 1880 census). (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81; 1880 U.S. census)
  • Corbett, Mrs. M. B. (born about 1834) Follower of Davies
    On Davies’s list of members in March 1881, aged 47; “Mary Corbet” in the 1880 census. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81; 1880 U.S. census)
  • Courtney, A. J. (at the camp 1876–77) Follower of Davies
    At the camp from about 1876 to July 1877; a plaintiff against Davies, awarded $400. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81)
  • Degroot, Mrs. Follower of Davies
    Credited with gifts of $218, 1869–78. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81, list of donations)
  • Evans, Thomas W. (at the camp 1870–78) Follower of Davies Earlier a Morrisite (probable)
    A plaintiff against Davies, awarded $2,700, the largest share. Davies said he came in 1870 and left in the spring of 1878. A Davies letter of March 1877 to a T. B. Evans, who was blind, looked for him “to come with Mr. Livingstone,” the roll’s R. J. Livingstone of Carson Valley. Emma Evans also testified. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81)
  • Hagg, Ola (born about 1808) Follower of Davies
    Born in Sweden; at the camp from July 1868, and a witness for Davies in 1881. Probably the father of the roll’s Svend Hegg (Sven Olasson). (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81; 1880 U.S. census)
  • Hollenbeck, Mrs. Follower of Davies
    Credited with gifts of $195, 1869–74. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81, list of donations)
  • Jones, Rachel D. (born about 1833) Follower of Davies
    Mrs. John E. Jones, born in Wales; a member from 1866, she came to Walla Walla with Davies in 1867. Her children were born in Utah and Montana. Her husband, sent out as a missionary in 1868 and later at Indian Valley, may be the roll’s apostle John E. Jones; that is not settled. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81; 1880 U.S. census)
  • Kelley, Michael H. (born about 1843) Follower of Davies
    A sailor, born in Ireland; first came to the camp in November 1868, having learned the doctrine at San Francisco. Testified for Davies in 1881. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81; 1880 U.S. census)
  • Lambert, V. D. (at the camp 1868–70) Follower of Davies
    A cooper and later a justice of the peace; Davies’s missionary at San Francisco in 1869–70. He left the community and testified against Davies. Sarah Lambert also testified. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81)
  • Miller, Søren (born about 1817) Follower of Davies Earlier a Morrisite (probable)
    “Sorn Miller,” born in Denmark, at the camp from July 1868 with his wife and children Christian J., John M. and Christina, who are all on Davies’s 1881 list. Klemgard’s testimony implies that the elder Miller had been with him in Utah or at Soda Springs, that is, among the Morrisites. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81; 1880 U.S. census)
  • Mortensen, James (born about 1825) Follower of Davies
    “J. Mortezen,” a shoemaker born in Sweden, at the camp from July 1868 with his wife Maria; their daughter Maria and Oscar Olsen are on the 1881 list. Whether the family connects to the Mortensens on the roll is not known. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81; 1880 U.S. census)
  • Parker, Mrs. E. S. J. (born about 1824) Follower of Davies (probable)
    A widow, a member from 1868, at San Francisco. Probably the widow of Thomas Parker, Davies’s missionary there; whether he is the Thomas Parker on the roll is not settled. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81)
  • Parker, Mary B. Follower of Davies
    “Mrs. Parker of San Francisco,” credited with gifts of $2,363.40, 1868–77. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81, list of donations)
  • Perkins, Cornelia S. (born about 1850) Follower of Davies
    Born in New Hampshire; a member from 1869 at San Francisco, at the camp from that fall. She took the minutes of meetings in shorthand, helped print the community’s tracts and taught the district school; later Davies’s second wife. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81; 1880 U.S. census; Find a Grave)
  • Perkins, M. A. C. (born about 1826) Follower of Davies
    Cornelia’s mother, a member from 1869 at San Francisco, at the camp from 1872. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81)
  • Roberts, Jane S. (born about 1811) Follower of Davies
    Born in England; at the camp from 1868, with her daughter Emily J. Roberts. Testified for Davies in 1881. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81; 1880 U.S. census)
  • Roberts, Robert (died January 1881) Follower of Davies
    Husband of Jane S. Roberts; Davies’s missionary at San Francisco and Portland, who preached the doctrine and administered the vow. He was later expelled from the community and did not return. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81)
  • Smith, Mrs. Follower of Davies
    Credited with gifts of $122. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81, list of donations)
  • Turnbull Follower of Davies
    Named with Robert Roberts as administering the vow at San Francisco about 1869. Given name not recorded. (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81)
  • Wood, S. W. (born about 1823) Follower of Davies
    A photographer, born in England, who had an upholstery shop in Portland; he heard of Davies at San Francisco in 1868, came to the camp in the fall of 1869, served as a missionary and is credited with $5,921.65 on the list of donations. A defendant in the 1880 suit (“Samuel Woods” in the 1880 census). (Cilley et al. v. Davies, 1880–81; 1880 U.S. census)
