Bibliography

Primary texts and scholarship, in Chicago style.

Primary texts

  • Articles of Faith. San Francisco: Committee of the Firstborn, 1887.
  • Bear, John L. (Johann Lebrecht Bär). “The Autobiography of John L. Bear.” Journal of History (Lamoni, IA), 1911–12. Anderson, Revisited, cites pp. 207–8 for Bear’s account of Morris’s death. A biographical sketch appears in Inez Smith Davis, The Story of the Church.
  • Brighouse, James, and Lars Peterson. The Voice at Midnight. Independence, MO, December 25, 1884. Four-page tract of the Order of Enoch.
  • Brighouse, James, and Henry I. Doremus. The Voice of the Seventh Angel! Salt Lake City and South Cottonwood, UT, 1887–1892. Five parts. HathiTrust · Google Books. Its title takes up Morris’s claim to be the Seventh Angel; the transcription checked for this guide does not name Morris.
  • Connor, P. Edward. Report to Lt. Col. R. C. Drum, June 2, 1863, on the expedition from Camp Douglas to Soda Springs, May 5–30, 1863. In The War of the Rebellion: A Compilation of the Official Records of the Union and Confederate Armies, ser. 1, vol. 50, pt. 1, 226–29. Washington, DC: Government Printing Office, 1897. The founding of Morristown and the removal of Morrisites to Carson City. OSU eHistory
  • Dove, George S., and James Dove. A Voice from the West. San Francisco: Church of the Firstborn, 1879.
  • Dove, James. A Few Items in the History of the Morrisites. San Francisco: Church of the Firstborn, 1892.
  • Eardley, John R. Gems of Inspiration: A Collection of Sublime Thoughts by Modern Prophets. San Francisco: Joseph A. Dove, printer, 1899. Internet Archive
  • Fuller, Frank, Acting Governor of Utah Territory. Order to Gen. Daniel H. Wells, June 11, 1862, calling out the militia as a posse to arrest Joseph Morris. Copy in a letter from Nauvoo Legion headquarters, January 14, 1871. Utah Territorial Militia Records, series 2210, document 1,215. Utah State Archives, Salt Lake City. FamilySearch image (free account).
  • Gibbs, Josiah F. Lights and Shadows of Mormonism. Salt Lake City: Salt Lake Tribune Publishing, 1909. Includes an account of the Morrisite War.
  • Gilbert, Frank T. Historic Sketches of Walla Walla, Whitman, Columbia and Garfield Counties, Washington Territory, and Umatilla County, Oregon. Portland, 1882. Includes an interview with William W. Davies.
  • Hickman, Bill. Brigham’s Destroying Angel: Life, Confessions and Startling Disclosures. Salt Lake City: Shepard Book Company, 1904.
  • Morris, Joseph. The Spirit Prevails: Containing the Revelations, Articles and Letters Written by Joseph Morris. San Francisco: George S. Dove and Company, 1886. Internet Archive
  • Smith, Joseph, III. Joseph Smith III and the Restoration. Edited by Mary Audentia Smith Anderson; condensed by Bertha Audentia Anderson Hulmes. Independence, MO: Herald House, 1952. Pages 432–33 describe his visit to the site of the Morrisite camp with John Parson on July 29, 1889; p. 437, Morrisite families at Soda Springs.
  • Stenhouse, Fanny (Mrs. T. B. H.). An Englishwoman in Utah: The Story of a Life’s Experience in Mormonism. London: Sampson Low, Marston, Searle & Rivington, 1880. The chapter “Killing a Rival Prophet” covers the Morrisites, with Alexander Dow’s affidavit and the 1863 sentences. Project Gutenberg
  • Stenhouse, T. B. H. The Rocky Mountain Saints: A Full and Complete History of the Mormons. New York: D. Appleton, 1873.
  • Watson, Kirk, ed. The Morrisites: Writings and Revelations. Writings of Joseph Morris, George Williams, William W. Davies, George S. Dove, and James Dove. Independently published, 2026. New

Books

  • Anderson, C. LeRoy. For Christ Will Come Tomorrow: The Saga of the Morrisites. Logan: Utah State University Press, 1981.
  • Anderson, C. LeRoy. Joseph Morris and the Saga of the Morrisites. 2nd ed. Logan: Utah State University Press, 1988.
  • Anderson, C. LeRoy. Joseph Morris and the Saga of the Morrisites (Revisited). Logan: Utah State University Press, 2010. Buy from the publisher · Digital edition (member libraries) Final edition
  • Arbaugh, George. Revelation in Mormonism. Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1932.
  • Ardent, John. Over the Wall of Oppression. Self-published, 1995.
  • Black, Susan Easton, comp. Early Members of the Reorganized Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints. 6 vols. Provo, UT: Religious Studies Center, Brigham Young University, 1993. Members born before 1849, with sources; the entries for Margaretta Cook and Lena Hanson record their years as Morrisites before joining the RLDS Church. FamilySearch Digital Library (free account)
  • Bringhurst, Newell G., and John C. Hamer, eds. Scattering of the Saints: Schism within Mormonism. Independence, MO: John Whitmer Books, 2007.
  • Chase, Neal. Ezekiel's Temple in Montana. Self-published, 1990.
  • Frazer, Robert W. Forts of the West: Military Forts and Presidios and Posts Commonly Called Forts West of the Mississippi River to 1898. Norman: University of Oklahoma Press, 1965. Camp Connor.
  • Jensen, Leland. The Seventh Angel Sounded. Missoula, MT: Acme Press, 1994.
  • Kraut, Ogden. The One Mighty and Strong. Salt Lake City: Pioneer Press, 1991. 137 pp. Its survey of claimants (83–100) includes James Brighouse (91–92), page references as cited by Brian C. Hales and by Shields; the paperback itself not examined. The shorter paper of the same title that Kraut gave at the Sunstone Symposium in August 1991 is online; its note on Brighouse (p. 10) does not mention Morris.
  • Launius, Roger D., and Linda Thatcher, eds. Differing Visions: Dissenters in Mormon History. Urbana: University of Illinois Press, 1994. See Holzapfel under Articles.
  • Madsen, Brigham D. Glory Hunter: A Biography of Patrick Edward Connor. Salt Lake City: University of Utah Press, 1990.
  • Reid, Agnes Just. Letters of Long Ago. Tanner Trust Fund ed. Introduction by Brigham D. Madsen, xi–xviii. Salt Lake City: University of Utah Library, 1973. Letters of Emma Thompson Just, a Morrisite and original settler of Soda Springs.
  • Reid, Agnes Just. The Lost Letters. Boise: Cedar Creek Press, 2006.
  • Seegmiller, Janet Burton. Be Kind to the Poor: The Life History of Robert Taylor Burton. Robert Taylor Burton Family Organization, 1988.
  • Shields, Steven L. Divergent Paths of the Restoration: An Encyclopedia of the Smith–Rigdon Movement. 5th ed. Salt Lake City: Signature Books, 2021. “Order of Enoch” (§5.4) and James Brighouse’s later Salt Lake City group (§5.4.1); the entry does not mention Morris. Google Books. Earlier editions include the 3rd ed., rev. and enl. (Bountiful, UT: Restoration Research, 1982), where the Order of Enoch is at p. 106, as cited by Wikipedia; not examined.
  • Varley, James. Brigham and the Brigadier: General Patrick Connor and His California Volunteers in Utah and Along the Overland Trail. Tucson: Westernlore Press, 1989.
