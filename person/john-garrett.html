John Garrett (1815–1899)

Probably the Gloucestershire shoemaker who crossed to Utah in 1853, lived at Moroni beside other Morrisite families, and ended his life near Blackfoot, Idaho, where he took up land under four different federal land laws.

Life

  1. 11 Jun 1815Born at Gloucester, England. (Family Tree)
  2. 1 Jan 1845Marries Finetta Nancy White at Bedminster, Somerset. (Family Tree)
  3. 1851A shoemaker in Gloucestershire. (1851 census of England and Wales, in Family Tree)
  4. 1853Crosses to Utah in the Joseph W. Young Company with his children Richard and Heneage. Finetta dies the same year. (Church History Biographical Database; Family Tree)
  5. 1860A shoemaker at Moroni, Sanpete County, Utah, with real estate worth $200 and personal property worth $250. The household is Thurza, 55, Richard, 14, Phenetta E., 12, and Amelia, 10 (Heneage Amelia). Niels R. Beck, also on the roll, is on the same census page. (1860 U.S. census)
  6. 1861–62John, Thurza, Fenetta, Hennage, Richard and a Jane Garrett are on the roll of membership. John is not on the list of men brought before Judge Kinney after the siege (Deseret News, June 25, 1862).
  7. 1870Probably the “Jno. Garrett,” 50, born in England, a fur trader, with Martha, 50, at a mining camp in the Snake River district of Oneida County, Idaho, enumerated August 4, 1870. His daughters were in the next district, Upper Snake River, counted by the same enumerator, A. J. Borland, a week earlier: Fenetta, married to F. S. Stephens, and next door Heneage, entered as a 19-year-old man working at mining (read as “Hemage Jarret” and, by the USGenWeb transcribers, “Honage Ganet”). (1870 U.S. census)
  8. 1874His son Richard dies in Idaho. (Family Tree)
  9. 1880Living at Blackfoot, then in Oneida County, Idaho, with his wife Thirza: John, 65, and Thirza, 75, both born in England. (1880 U.S. census)
  10. 1882–92Receives four federal land patents near Blackfoot, all in the same township (Boise Meridian, T3S R35E), each under a different law: 158.45 acres by cash entry (January 20, 1882, Oxford land office); a 160-acre homestead (April 18, 1888, Oxford); 200 acres under the Desert Land Act of 1877, which required the claimant to irrigate the land (September 23, 1890, Blackfoot land office); and a Timber Culture Act claim, granted for planting trees (March 17, 1892). (BLM General Land Office records)
  11. 9 May 1899Dies at Blackfoot, Idaho. (Family Tree)

Thurza, not Martha

The roll names John’s wife Thurza, and the 1860 census at Moroni has Thurza, aged 55. The 1870 census in Oneida County gives the wife of “Jno. Garrett” as Martha, aged 50, which raised the question of a second wife. The 1880 census answers it: John Garrett, 65, and his wife Thirza, 75, were living together at Blackfoot. The 1870 “Martha” was most likely the enumerator’s mistake.

The land at Blackfoot

John’s four patents lie close together in one township near Blackfoot, more than 500 acres in all. Using cash entry, the Homestead Act, the Desert Land Act and the Timber Culture Act together was a common way to put together a larger farm than any one law allowed. A homestead normally needed five years’ residence before final proof, so John was probably living on that land by about 1883, when he was in his late sixties. The case files at the National Archives would show when he made each entry and who witnessed his proofs.
