The Montana church

Peter Staffanson (1823–1913)

A Swedish shoemaker who came to Utah as a Latter-day Saint in 1855, joined the Morrisites in Iowa in 1869, as George Williams organized the church at Council Bluffs, and moved to Montana in 1872. He presided over a quorum of Seventies, then served as bishop of William James’s English-speaking Stake No. 1, and stood with James in the presidency dispute of 1886. He farmed on Lost Creek near Anaconda for forty years, and his family and Andrew Hendrikson’s were joined by marriage.

Not on the roll
He joined in 1869, after the 1861–62 roll was drawn up. See his entry under Others named.
Name
Pehr Staffansson in Swedish; written Staffenson, Staffensen, Steffanson, Stoffenson and Stevenson in the records, and Staffanson by his family
Born
November 22, 1823, in Skåne, Sweden (Council Bluffs record of members, MS 32226; 1900 U.S. census); family records name the village as Östra Torn, near Lund
Came to America
1855, on the James Nesmith, Liverpool to New Orleans, with his wife and three children (Saints by Sea). Their son Nels Peter is entered as born in Denmark in the 1860, 1870 and 1880 censuses, which suggests the family lived in Denmark before they sailed
Married
Cecilia Nilsson (1827–1897), at Lund in 1851 (family records)
Children
Ten: Emma Caroline (Mrs. Oliver Barrett), Sophia Wilhelmina (died at sea, 1855), Nels Peter, Ephraim, Joseph, Hiram, Jacob, Nephi (died in infancy), John and William Henry
Church offices
President at Crescent City, Iowa, 1870; president of the second quorum of Seventies in Montana, 1872–78; first counselor to Bishop Rasmus Rasmussen, 1875; bishop of Stake No. 1 by 1883 and in 1886
Died
June 16, 1913, at his ranch on Lost Creek, Deer Lodge County, aged 89; buried in the Staffanson family cemetery there, beside his wife

Life

  1. 22 Nov 1823Born in Skåne, Sweden. (MS 32226)
  2. 1851–52Marries Cecilia Nilsson at Lund; the two are baptized Latter-day Saints at Copenhagen on December 4, 1852. (RoLayne Rich Staffanson, “From Sturdy Stock,” 2008, citing the Swedish Mission records)
  3. 1854–55Leaves Copenhagen in November 1854, a few days after the birth of his son Nels Peter, and sails from Liverpool on January 7, 1855, on the James Nesmith with 440 Scandinavian Saints under Peter O. Hansen; the register lists him as a shoemaker, aged 31. His daughter Sophia dies at sea on January 27. The family crosses the plains in the Secrist company and reaches Great Salt Lake City on September 7, 1855. (Saints by Sea; Hansen’s diary and the crossing records, as given in “From Sturdy Stock”)
  4. 1857–66Lives at Brigham City, Box Elder County, Utah, where six sons are born; the 1860 census enumerates the family as “Peter Stevenson.” (1860 U.S. census, Brigham City, p. 199)
  5. Feb 1869At Council Bluffs, on February 1, he is present when George Williams (“Cainan”) ordains Søren Peter Guhl an apostle; on February 2 he is baptized into the Morrisite church at Crescent City, Iowa; on February 3 Williams washes his feet and tells him that his birthright is that of a first son of Adam, with “much to do with the bishop’s office.” (MS 32226, Council Bluffs record of members; Danish record book, Omaha and Council Bluffs, 1869, MS 31876; translation from the Danish generated by Claude)
  6. 1869–71With Andrew Hendrikson he baptizes converts at Crescent City, among them Lars and Maren Jørgensen (April 13, 1869) and Carrie Jensen, Hendrikson’s future wife (October 16, 1871). In March 1870 the conference at Omaha makes him president at Crescent City. (MS 32226; Omaha minutebook, MS 31876)
  7. 29 Aug 1869A son, Hendrik Nils Vilhelm, is born; he is blessed on January 8, 1870. (blessing slip, Church History Library, MS 31876, Member records, circa 1869–1890)
  8. 1870Enumerated at Crescent Township, Pottawattamie County, Iowa, farming, with his wife and nine children. (1870 U.S. census, Crescent Township)
  9. 22 Sep 1872When the Montana church is organized at William P. Thomas’s house on Warm Spring Creek, he is appointed to preside over the second quorum of Seventies. The family has come to the upper Deer Lodge Valley that year and settles on Lost Creek. A family letter of 1995 says the Staffansons and the Hendricksons came to the valley together that year with a wagon train from Utah, the Staffansons settling at Willow Glen near Anaconda, and his great-grandson Robert Staffanson’s memoir also has the families coming north from Utah by wagon train in 1872; the church’s own minutebook says the emigration came “from the States of Iowa and Nebraska,” beginning in the summer of 1871, so the families probably went west by rail and north by wagon. (minutebook, September 1872–January 1874, MS 32226; see the Chronology; obituary, 1913; Bob [Robert] Staffanson, “To Cousins in the Hendrickson and Staffanson Families,” typescript letter, April 16, 1995; copy shared by Kristin Campbell, daughter of Robert Staffanson and a great-granddaughter of Andrew Hendrikson, and used with her permission; Witness to Spirit, 2015)
  10. 1873–77Sits on the Danish-speaking council at Warm Spring and Deer Lodge. In July 1873 he and Emanuel Strom visit Nels Damander and find him opposed to Williams. In June 1875 Bishop Rasmussen chooses him as first counselor, as the only one present “who has been under the Lord’s Servant’s administration.” In 1877 Williams writes that he may go with William James “to open in another locality,” and in September Staffanson tells the council that James had been offended because Danish was spoken. (council minutes, 1873, 1875, 1877, MS 31876; Williams to James, August 16, 1877, MS 31876)
  11. 1878Goes over to James’s Stake No. 1 “on the strength of what the Lord’s servant had written,” without first asking his own stake, and on June 2 is released as president of the Seventies. (council minutes, April 7 and June 2, 1878, MS 31876)
  12. 1879–80Williams hears in January 1879 that “Bro. Staffenson has a waggon & wishes to ride me round.” In September 1880 he answers at length Staffanson’s question about an “inconsistency” in his teaching on the Fall: “Now Br. Steffanson dwell on the word and ask the Comforter.” (Williams to James, January 6, 1879, and to Hendrickson, September 19, 1880, MS 31876; see Teachings)
  13. 1880Farming in the Deer Lodge Valley with his wife and five sons; his sons Ephraim and Nels Peter live next door with their wives Elizabeth and Charlotte, daughters of Daniel James. (1880 U.S. census, Deer Lodge Valley, enumeration district 11, p. 11)
  14. 1883–84As bishop of Stake No. 1 he is seated in the Court of the Lord’s House. In November 1883 he reports to the court on his visit, with Strom, to Rasmussen, who refused to give up the church records and the Reserve Fund; in May 1884 he warns that “many spirits” are abroad and that the Saints may be led astray by the gifts. (minutebook, 1881–1890, MS 32226, pp. 13–18)
  15. 1886With William M. James and Daniel James, the officers of Stake No. 1, he refuses to accept George Thompson as president and sustains James instead; John R. Eardley reports that while rumor had “John the Revelator” coming, old Staffanson kept a team ready to fetch him. (Eardley to Hendrikson, May 2, 1886, MS 31876)
  16. 1894After the division of the early 1890s, Eardley hears that “Staffanson & Mrs. Daniel James” have agreed to let his group use the meeting house part of the time. (Eardley to Hendrikson, March 28, 1894, MS 31876)
  17. 4 Mar 1897Cecilia dies; she is later reburied in the family cemetery on Lost Creek. (Find a Grave memorial 201747978; “From Sturdy Stock”)
  18. 23 Oct 1897His son Jacob and Jacob’s wife Glendora are baptized into the Reorganized Church at Anaconda by A. B. Moore; their daughters follow in 1907. (Record of the Deer Lodge Branch, Montana District, #228, Community of Christ Library Archives, nos. 119, 124, 145–146)
  19. 1900–10Widowed, he owns his farm east of Anaconda in 1900; the Lost Creek water decree of 1904 gives him 231 inches; in 1910, aged 86, he lives with his son Jacob at Lost Creek. (1900 U.S. census, Anaconda Township, enumeration district 14, sheet 5A; Anaconda Standard, December 31, 1904, p. 5; 1910 U.S. census, Lost Creek, enumeration district 8, sheet 1A)
  20. 16 Jun 1913Dies at the ranch on Lost Creek, a pioneer of the valley since 1872; the funeral is held at the family home on June 18. He is buried beside Cecilia in the family cemetery, under an uncarved granite stone. (Butte Daily Post, June 16, 1913, p. 8; Anaconda Standard, June 18, 1913, p. 5; a 1971 Anaconda newspaper article quoted in “From Sturdy Stock”)
  21. 1913–18His estate goes almost wholly to his son Jacob: about 280 acres on Lost Creek (S½ SE¼ and NW¼ SE¼ of section 19 and W½ NE¼ of section 20, with an eleven-twelfths share of the E½ NW¼ of section 20, T5N R10W), and the Lost Creek water rights confirmed to “Peter Staffenson” by the court decree of December 30, 1904: 115.95 miner’s inches appropriated as of June 1870 and 115.2 inches as of 1877. Each of his eight surviving children was heir to an eighth: Emma Caroline, Nels Peter, Ephraim, Joseph, Hiram, Jacob, John T. and William Henry. In 1914 Jacob bought out the other seven. Emma Caroline Barritt, still living at Council Bluffs, and Joseph and his wife Minnie sold him their shares in March, and John T. and his wife Nannie A., of Anaconda, sold theirs on July 11, each for $400; the deeds were recorded together on September 4, 1914. The deeds left out one forty-acre piece, the E½ NW¼ of section 20, and a corrective deed was signed by Ephraim and Elizabeth A., Hiram and Mary, Joseph and Minnie, William H. and Jennie, Emma Caroline Barrett, N. P. and Lillie, and Nannie A. Staffanson. By then John T. had died, in 1915, without a will; his estate was distributed on February 9, 1916, one-third to his widow and two-thirds to his five minor children, Eugene, Clarence, Martha, Stanford and Pearl. When the probate court distributed Peter’s estate on August 30, 1918, it gave Jacob the land “as a son of said deceased, and as the grantee and assignee of all the other heirs,” with eleven-twelfths of that forty acres; each of John T.’s children received a one-sixtieth share. No federal patent in his name covers this land: section 19 was railroad-grant land and the parts of section 20 had been patented to others between 1872 and 1889, so he bought the farm, and its 1870 water right, older than his arrival in 1872, came with the land. (estate of Peter Staffanson, decree of distribution, August 30, 1918, pp. 4–6, Deer Lodge County Probate Court, “Deer Lodge, Montana, United States records,” FamilySearch, image group 007660240, images 557–559; deeds nos. 2536–2538, March 26 and 31 and July 11, 1914, recorded September 4, 1914, Deer Lodge County Recorder, deed record pp. 48–52, “Deer Lodge, Montana, United States records,” FamilySearch, image group 008581860, images 76–77; Find a Grave, memorial 118257713, John T. Staffanson, Upper Hill Cemetery, Anaconda; Bureau of Land Management, General Land Office Records, T5N R10W, sections 19–20)

Between two stakes

Staffanson was one of the few Scandinavians in the Montana church who had met George Williams in person, at Council Bluffs in 1869, and Bishop Rasmussen chose him in 1875 for that reason. He belonged at first to Andrew Hendrikson’s Danish-speaking council, but in 1878 he went over to William James’s English-speaking stake, citing a letter of Williams’s, and he stayed with James through the disputes after Williams’s death. His family ties ran the same way: his sons Ephraim and Nels Peter married Elizabeth and Charlotte James, daughters of Daniel James, the Welshman who had gone out with William P. Thomas in 1869 and who served as counselor and teacher in the Montana church (Find a Grave memorials 135930961 and 107142416). In the next generation the line crossed back: Ephraim’s sons George and Frank married Julia and Agnes Hendrickson, daughters of Andrew Hendrikson.

Records and family history

The church records name him in Danish and English minutes from 1869 to 1886 and in Eardley’s letters to 1894. His family’s history, written by RoLayne Rich Staffanson (“From Sturdy Stock: The Staffansons of Sweden,” 1997, revised 2008), follows him from Lund to Brigham City and Lost Creek, with the 1855 voyage from Peter O. Hansen’s diary, but does not mention the Morrisites; neither does his obituary. A portrait of him and a photograph of him with his seven sons, shared by descendants, can be seen in his Memories on FamilySearch (KWJK-LWP); they are not reproduced here.

Still open

Why the family left the Latter-day Saints in Utah and went to Crescent City: they were still in Utah in October 1866, when their son John was born, and in Iowa by 1869, when William Henry was born there (1870 and 1880 censuses); whether “Bro. Stevenson,” the tinner who made a coffin for Captain Secrist in July 1855, was he; and the paper and date of the 1971 Anaconda article on the Lost Creek cemetery.
