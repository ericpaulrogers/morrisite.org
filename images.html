Cannonballs

Cannonballs associated with the 1862 siege of Kington Fort. Soda Springs, Idaho

The grave of Niels and Mary Christoffersen Anderson, survivors of the 1862 siege and among the first settlers of Morristown.

Transcription of the Anderson plaque (MO-IMG-061)

NIELS ANDERSON  ·  OCTOBER 3 [?], 1835 – MARCH 4 [?], 1926
MARY CHRISTOFFERSEN ANDERSON  ·  JULY 26, 1847 – JULY 13 [?], 1928

THEY WERE BORN IN DENMARK; JOINED THE MORMONS THERE. IN 1857 MRS. ANDERSON ARRIVED AT BURLINGTON, IOWA; DETAINED THERE UNTIL 1859 ON ACCOUNT OF JOHNSTON’S ARMY HAVING TO BE SENT TO UTAH. THAT YEAR SHE WALKED NEARLY ALL THE WAY TO SALT LAKE CITY. IN 1860 MR. ANDERSON CAME; HE PULLED A HAND-CART FROM FLORENCE, NEBRASKA. THEY SOON SAW THE DECEPTION IN “BRIGHAMISM,” JOINED MORRIS A SECEDER FROM BRIGHAM. MAY 22 [?], 1863, THEY CAME TO SODA SPRINGS WITH THE FIRST SETTLERS; THEY WERE ESCORTED AND PROTECTED BY GEN. CONNOR AND CALIFORNIA VOLUNTEERS. JULY 20, 1863, THEY WERE MARRIED HERE; THEIR MARRIAGE WAS THE FIRST IN IDAHO TERRITORY AS FAR AS THEY KNEW. LATER MR. ANDERSON BECAME “THE FATHER OF THE ANTI-MORMON PARTY” IN IDAHO.

AT SOUTH WEBER, UTAH, JUNE 13–15, 1862, MR. AND MRS. ANDERSON WERE IN “THE MORRISITE MASSACRE.” MRS. ANDERSON’S CHIN WAS SHOT AWAY WITH A CANNON BALL, THAT KILLED MRS. BARBARA DE[…]THELM [?] AND MRS. JOSEPH (?) MARSH, FIRED BY GEN. ROBERT T. BURTON’S MORMON MILITIA INTO A BOWERY OF UNARMED “MORRISITES,” JUNE 13, BECAUSE THEY REFUSED TO OBEY “BRIGHAM’S” DECREES, AFTER JOHNSTON’S ARMY HAD LEFT UTAH ON ACCOUNT OF THE CIVIL WAR. MRS. J. L. BEAR AND HER BABY WERE KILLED WITH ANOTHER CANNON BALL. THEN MANY MORRISITES WITH THEIR OLD GUNS TRIED RESISTANCE. ON THE THIRD DAY ALL SURRENDERED UNDER A WHITE FLAG.

MRS. ANDERSON SAYS: “WHEN MORRIS CAME BEFORE GEN. BURTON, THE LATTER USED ABUSIVE LANGUAGE AND TRIED TO RIDE OVER MORRIS ON HIS HORSE. THIS ABUSE WAS ON ACCOUNT OF HIS RELIGIOUS TEACHING. I SAW HIM … SHOOT MORRIS.”

DOW’S AFFIDAVIT SAYS: BURTON SHOT MORRIS, BANKS, MRS. JAMES BOWMAN AND ANOTHER WOMAN, AFTER THEY SURRENDERED. TEN “MORRISITES” WERE KILLED, AND ONE “MORMON.” THE PRISONERS WERE TO BE TRIED AT THE NEXT TERM OF COURT HELD BY CHIEF JUSTICE KINNEY, A MORMON. IN THE MEANTIME GOV. HARDING, ASSOCIATE JUSTICES WAITE, DRAKE, COL. CONNOR AND VOLUNTEERS ARRIVED.

“SEVEN MORRISITES” WERE SENTENCED TO IMPRISONMENT FROM “TEN TO FIFTEEN YEARS.” “SIXTY-SIX” HEAVILY FINED.

“WE,” WROTE GOV. HARDING IN 1874, “ATTENDED THROUGH THE TRIAL, WHICH WAS NOTHING BUT A MOCKERY. BURTON ADMITTED HIS SHOOTING THE PRISONERS. … HAD I BEEN ON THE BENCH I SHOULD HAVE HAD HIM ARRESTED … ; BUT IT WOULD HAVE BEEN USELESS. THE JURORS WOULD ALL BE MORMONS, AND RECOGNIZE NO LAW BUT ‘THE COMMANDS OF AUTHORITY.’

“PETITIONS WERE GOTTEN UP FOR THE UNCONDITIONAL PARDON OF THE MORRISITES, WHICH WERE SIGNED BY ALL THE GENTILES, … NOT A MORMON SIGNED THEM. …

“I ISSUED THE PARDON; … SOME MOUNTED MORTARS … COMMANDING THE BEE-HIVE HOUSE AND LION HOUSE, MADE THINGS TOLERABLY SMOOTH ON THE SURFACE.”

BRIGHAM YOUNG JUNE 15, AS BY H. P. McLANE, SAID, “THAT MORRIS AND BANKS WILL BE TAKEN I HAVE NO DOUBT. … BUT IF I HAD MY OWN WAY ABOUT IT, I WOULD NOT SPARE NEITHER MAN, WOMAN NOR CHILD; BUT AS IT IS A UNITED STATES AFFAIR, THEY CAN HAVE IT AS THEY PLEASE. BUT AS REGARDS THIS YOUNG MAN, HE HAS DIED A MARTYR TO THE CAUSE AND KINGDOM OF OUR GOD. … THIS WAS A MATTER OF NECESSITY. … THE WORST … I [?] DISLIKE … , IS THAT AFTER THEY ARE ROUTED, THE … ACCURSED DEVILS … WILL BE TRYING TO SNEAK BACK INTO THE CHURCH AGAIN. …”

R. W. YOUNG WRITING FOR THE “CONTRIBUTOR,” OF 1890, FOR THE MORMON MUTUAL IMPROVEMENT ASSOCIATION SAYS: “THE … ACTION OF THE GOVERNOR WAS FOLLOWED … IN COURSE OF TIME, BY PERSECUTION OF GEN. BURTON. … A GRAND JURY WAS LED IN … 1870, TO BRING IN AN INDICTMENT FOR MURDER … , AND … (HE) THOUGHT HIMSELF COMPELLED BY CIRCUMSTANCES TO EVADE ARREST … LATER, ANOTHER INDICTMENT WAS FOUND AGAINST GEN. BURTON … , HE MADE NO ATTEMPT TO ESCAPE. …”

BETWEEN THESE TWO INDICTMENTS A RULING HAD “PROVIDED IN SUBSTANCE FOR JURIES HALF MORMON, AND HALF GENTILE.”

THIS YOUNG SAYS, “THE PROSECUTION LABORED LONG, AND,” “CLOUDS OF WITNESSES WERE BROUGHT FORTH,” “TO PERSUADE THE JURY,” “ONE-HALF BEING NON-MORMONS,” THAT BURTON KILLED MRS. BOWMAN.

THIS CASE WAS NO. 3109, U.S. VS. BURTON FOR “MURDER 1ST DEGREE,” SET FOR TRIAL 1ST DAY OF FEB. TERM 1879, MARCH 8 — INDICTMENT DISMISSED. THIS WAS ONE OF THE THREE INDICTMENTS AGAINST HIM.

IT WAS ASKED: WHAT ABOUT THE CASE FOR KILLING MORRIS? U.S. DISTRICT ATTORNEY VANZILE ANSWERED “WHAT IS THE USE, IF THEY WILL NOT CONVICT FOR KILLING AN INNOCENT WOMAN?”

TO FATHER AND MOTHER, AND HISTORIC TO MAN, WE DEDICATE THIS MEMORIAL.

ABRAHAM CHRISTOPHER ANDERSON, I[D]A COLLETT, ANNA CHRISTINA ROGERS,
DAVID ANDREW ANDERSON, ABELONE LARSEN, PETER WILLIAM ANDERSON,
NIELS CHRISTIAN ANDERSON, MARY AMELIA VERHAM.

Transcribed by the editor from MO-IMG-061 (October 2018), keeping the plaque’s capitals and punctuation; paragraphs follow its indentations. Ellipses and the “(?)” after “Joseph” are on the plaque. Bracketed readings are the editor’s and are uncertain where the bronze is worn: the dates marked [?], the first victim’s surname (perhaps the Swiss name Diethelm), and Ida Collett’s first name. The plaque is the Anderson children’s account and quotes its sources selectively; compare the trial record and the histories in the Bibliography. Corrections from a clearer reading are welcome.

Racetrack church: condition record, September 5, 1989

The captions in all the Racetrack galleries name Deer Lodge County, the county the church was built in. It has been in Powell County since 1901. The earliest views in this record, and the fullest record of the furnished interior: the pulpit and railing, benches, chandelier, a Jewel Cottage stove, and pictures on the walls. The date, 9/5/89, is written on each sheet of the copies. The photographer is not recorded. The prints were photocopied, the photocopies were scanned, and these images are cut from that scan, so tones are harsh and fine detail is lost. Courtesy of Barbara B. Walden, Executive Director, Community of Christ Historic Sites Foundation, who shared the scan with the editor on May 25, 2023. If you know who took these photographs, or have the original prints, please write.

Racetrack church: condition record, before 2004

Summer views from the editor’s scanned prints. The shingle roof and the front siding are largely intact.

Racetrack church: condition record, February 19, 2009

Taken on one afternoon visit, these views record the building’s condition on that date: weathered and largely unpainted siding, boarded windows and doors, sections of lower siding missing with the framing exposed, and a shingle roof still in place. Compare the July 2012 and November 2018 records below.

Racetrack church: condition record and reunion visit, July 2012

Taken when the property owners allowed a reunion of Morrisite descendants, held in Deer Lodge, to visit the church. Outside, the shingle roof is complete and the siding below and beside the windows has begun to open up, between the 2009 and 2018 conditions. Inside, the room was still finished in painted beadboard, with the pulpit, railing, and a chair in place. Date provisional: these copies show only that the photographs were taken in or before July 2012.

Comparison: the front of the church, 2009 and 2018

Racetrack church: condition record, November 2018

Compared with 2009: much more siding has fallen from the front, leaving large areas of framing exposed, especially to the right of the doors and below both windows. On the unpainted gable end the siding has partly collapsed, with a long board hanging loose. Dark gaps in the snow on the roof suggest missing shingles. The painted rear gable end remains largely intact, the lettered panel is still in place, and the front doors are padlocked. Date provisional: these copies show only that the photographs were taken in or before November 2018.

Racetrack church: condition record, August 16, 2020

Photographs contributed by a reader. People who appear in some of the views are left out or cropped out. Inside, the benches, table, pulpit and runner are much as they were in 1989. Hymnals and a 1931 Reorganized Church lesson quarterly were still in the building. Outside, the siding has continued to fall, and one of the front doors stands open. The church is on private land.

Racetrack church: later views by others

Not reproduced here; listed so the condition record can be followed past 2020. Dates are YouTube posting dates: “Last Existing Morrisite Church in the World” (February 17, 2026) and Marques Vickers, “Montana Morrisite Church: The Dilapidated Remnant” (July 29, 2026).

People and portraits

Historical portraits from the editor’s files; their original sources were not recorded.

Portraits published in Anderson, Joseph Morris and the Saga of the Morrisites (Revisited) (2010), not reproduced here:

  • Joseph Morris, with a man captioned as his older brother William, dated about 1841, England (p. 118). The only photograph identified as Morris; on its date and identification, see Open questions.
  • A later portrait captioned as Mary Christoffersen Anderson, different from MO-IMG-062 (p. 119). The caption makes her seventeen when she was wounded; the birth date on her gravestone, July 26, 1847, makes her fourteen.
  • George Thompson, with two unidentified women, in a pony cart (p. 120). The caption calls him one of the first to join the Morrisite army and a later successor to George Williams, the Prophet Cainan; Anderson’s text dates his election as president of the church to May 1886.
  • John R. Eardley, George Williams (the Prophet Cainan), and George Thompson, England, about 1873, during a Morrisite mission there under Williams (p. 121).
  • George Johnson and Andrew Hendrikson (spelled Henrikson in the caption), Danish immigrants who led the Montana church (p. 124). The caption’s “from 1866” for Hendrikson’s presidency is a misprint; Anderson’s text gives 1886 to 1921. See Chronology.
