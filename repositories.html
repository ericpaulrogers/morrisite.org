Willow Glen photographs

Anaconda, Montana
Held by
Marcus Daly Museum and Archives, Anaconda, Montana, in the basement of the original city hall; kept by volunteers in cooperation with the Copper Village Museum and Art Center
Contents
Photographs of Willow Glen, east of Anaconda, among them two 1915 groups of the Rasmus Rasmussen family captioned “Morrisites” (010-10-000332 and 010-10-000333), a view of the Willow Glen Stock Farm, and fire damage at Willow Glen (print P-003605, negative N401139). In the museum’s numbers P marks a print, N a negative and G a glass negative. A fire caused by lightning destroyed barns at Willow Glen, then a ranch of the Mt. Haggin Livestock Company, on August 6, 1963 (Anaconda Standard, August 7, 1963, p. 1); the photographs are dated “1970’s.”
Access
Through the director of the Copper Village Museum and Art Center. The photographs are not reproduced in this guide. New

Eliza Eardley Hardin reminiscence

MTHS SC 788
Held by
Montana Historical Society Library & Archives, Helena, Montana
Extent
11 pages, typescript, signed; undated (received by the Society in 1952)
Contents
Hardin (born 1873) recalls her family’s journey by covered wagon from Salinas, California, in 1881 and their arrival that August at a crossroads east of the later Anaconda, where her father, “Mr. Eardley,” built a log store he named Willow Glen, which became a post office. The New North-West credits the Willow Glen store and post office to John R. Eardley (January–March 1882), so she was evidently his daughter. Family Tree agrees: it identifies her as Eliza Fitchford Eardley, born at Stockport, England, on February 25, 1873, a daughter of John Rowley Eardley and Sarah Jane Jackson of the 1861–62 roll, who married Andrew Evan Hardin at Santa Rosa, California, in 1899. It gives her death as September 21, 1969, at San Diego; the Society’s catalog has 1967 (FamilySearch K2TC-7BS). She describes “an old log church” near the crossroads and a William James living next to it, very probably the Morrisite leader of that name, though she never calls the people Morrisites. The church’s own minutes bear her out: the Montana council met at the Willow Glen meeting house in 1882, 1884 and 1885, the meeting place of the Warm Spring Creek stake under William James, and an Eliza F. Eardley voted at the general assembly of April 25, 1886 (Church History Library, MS 32226). See Deer Lodge Valley.
Access
Open. The Society scanned it for this guide in November 2026; small scans are provided on request. Cite as: Eliza Eardley Hardin, reminiscence, undated (received 1952). Eliza Eardley Hardin Reminiscence. SC 788. Montana Historical Society Library & Archives, Helena, Montana. New

Henry Hendrickson interview, April 19, 1962

UM OH 018-001
Held by
Archives and Special Collections, Mansfield Library, University of Montana, Missoula; Religion in Montana Oral History Collection
Extent
One audio recording (36 minutes) and a 28-page transcript
Contents
Henry Hendrickson, interviewed by Don Cornelius for his 1962 thesis on Mormonism in Montana (listed under Theses), describes the families, his own among them, who settled in the Deer Lodge Valley in the 1870s, and the Church of the Firstborn, or Morrisites. Hendrickson, born in the valley in 1877, was the son of Andrew Hendrikson, who led the Montana church until his death in 1921. He recalls that his father was never a Latter-day Saint, joined the group around Council Bluffs and Omaha, and came with it in 1872 by rail to Corinne and then by wagon, a party he put at seventy-five or eighty people (pp. 10–13, 19); that besides the church at Race Track there was another “up around Lost Creek,” near Anaconda, with settlers also around Warm Springs (pp. 1–2); that most were Scandinavians, Danes above all around Race Track (p. 3); that the Eliasons came from Soda Springs (p. 11); that John Eardley had gone with the California group and came back to Montana (pp. 4–5); that preaching was mostly from the Bible, sometimes from the Book of Mormon and the Doctrine and Covenants (pp. 20–21); that Latter-day Saint missionaries were sometimes allowed to speak in the Race Track church (p. 20); and that the church faded around 1919–20, the young people joining the Reorganized Church, with George Johnson, who lived to be 98, the last preacher (pp. 8–9, 18). He left the valley in 1908. The catalog spells the name Hendrickson; Anderson spells Andrew Hendrikson’s name without the c.
Access
The transcript is online; the library holds copyright, and permission may be needed to quote it. Catalog record and transcript New

Cilley, Evans and Courtney v. Davies, 1880

Frontier Justice WAL-3153
Held by
Washington State Archives, Eastern Region Branch, Cheney, Washington
Contents
Case file of the Washington Territorial District Court for Walla Walla County, original case no. 2373, 1880: a civil suit to collect wages and debts brought by Asa B. Cilley, Thomas W. Evans and A. J. Courtney against William W. Davies, Alfred Davies and W. Wood. The archives’ index calls it the “Walla Walla Jesus” case. The file runs to 292 pages. It holds depositions taken before a referee in 1880–81 by members and former members, among them Peter C. Klemgard, Swen O. Hagg and Broer Sorenson of the 1861–62 roll, and Davies himself; a list of the forty-three members in March 1881, with their ages; printed tracts, hymns and letters by Davies filed as exhibits, among them his printed “Synopsis of Remarks” at a meeting of January 2, 1876; lists of donations to the community from 1866 to 1878; the referee’s report; the judgment of May 25, 1881, for $3,225 (Evans $2,700, Courtney $400, Cilley $125), with interest and costs; and the sheriff’s returns: Davies’s livestock and equipment were sold on June 11, 1881, and the community’s eighty acres, the west half of the northwest quarter of section 34, township 7 north, range 37 east, on July 16, 1881.
Related case
First National Bank of Walla Walla v. William A. Davies, William W. Davies and Lewis H. Bowman, 1885 (WAL-4626, original no. 4262; 28 pages): nine promissory notes of November 14, 1879, totaling $1,770, made by William W. Davies and his son William A. Davies to Lewis H. Bowman, who sold them to the bank the same day. The bank sued in February 1885 and took judgment by default on May 21, 1885, for $4,141.60 with attorney’s fees. The son was served in Lincoln County, near Sprague. Zbaraschuk (1989) lists two more suits by the same bank in the territorial district court: case 3093 (1882), against William A. and William W. Davies, on promissory notes, ending in judgment for $4,259 with costs and interest; and case 3260 (1883), against William A. and William W. Davies, Grosvenor S. Andrus, D. S. Baker and John F. Boyer, which he says holds 188 pages of testimony, including Andrus’s and both Davieses’. Neither has been ordered for this guide. New
Access
Indexed in “Frontier Justice,” the Washington State Archives’ guide to the territorial court case files, 1853–1889 (index record, 1880; 1885). The papers are not online; copies may be ordered from the Eastern Region Branch, which scanned both files for this guide in November 2026. Pages 158–195, the depositions of five defense witnesses, were missing when the county microfilmed the file; Whitman College Library later found them among its own holdings and returned them, and they are now with the case file (notes by the Walla Walla County clerk and by the archivist, 2002, filed with the case). Pages 133–157 are not in the file. The archivist who scanned it for this guide confirms that the whole file, several folders, was scanned and sent, and that these pages were evidently already missing when it came to the archives; he suspects they left the courthouse the same way pages 158–195 reached Whitman, perhaps when the clerk’s office reported the records “checked out” (Lee Pierce, Washington State Archives, Eastern Region Branch, email to the editor, November 9, 2026). Kirk Watson reports that they are not in FamilySearch’s images of the county microfilm either (FamilySearch, sign-in required). Zbaraschuk (1989), working from that microfilm, already noted “major gaps” in the pagination. New Kirk Watson’s edition prints Davies’s own deposition in the 1880 case.

Diaries, letters, and federal records

Several repositories
Church History Library
Brigham Young correspondence; Journal of Wilford Woodruff; Journal History of the Church; “Niels Anderson matter, 1903” (Joseph F. Smith stake correspondence, CR 1 191; digitized), letters of January–March 1903 from Niels Anderson, one also signed by Mary Christoffersen Anderson, asking the church’s help for the injury Mary received in 1862, with a clerk’s draft for the reply and the Bannock Stake president’s report of May 19, 1903, on the First Presidency’s inquiry; George Morris reminiscences, circa 1867–1886 (MS 1614; catalog record), by Joseph Morris’s brother, with a family genealogy and incidents about Joseph. The manuscript opens “A Short sketch of the Geneoligy and History of George Morris” (p. 1). Two accounts by men called out against the Morrisites: Newton Tuttle’s diary, January 1859–June 1862, kept at Bountiful, which includes his service in the territorial militia during the Morrisite War (MS 4299 1; catalog record), and Benjamin C. Critchlow’s reminiscences, circa 1865, of Weber County, the Utah War and the Morrisite War (MS 1662, carbon copy of a typescript; catalog record). Neither is digitized, and neither has yet been read for this guide. New
University of Utah
Diary of Hans Olsen Magleby; journal of John L. Blyth, 1858–1863; pioneer journal of Jacob Miller; Timothy Kendell autobiography (MS 0041), recalling South Weber as briefly called Morristown.
Utah State Archives
Territorial militia records (series 2210; images and a name index on FamilySearch, collection 1462415): Acting Governor Fuller’s order of June 11, 1862, calling out the posse (copied in document 1,215); a list of the South Weber company’s officers, April 22, 1859; Weber District muster rolls of the 1857 call-up, which include Robert Farley and John Parson.
National Archives
Record Group 60, Department of Justice chronological files, Utah, box 683: Sumner Howard to Attorney General Charles Devens, July 28, 1877. Record Group 49, General Land Office land entry case files: the papers behind the federal land patents of Morrisite settlers, among them Erik Eliasson’s homestead (Helena land office, Homestead Certificate 560, patented 1888) and Niels R. Beck’s preemption entry (Helena, certificate 230, patented 1871), whose proofs should give the date each man settled on his land. Copies are ordered on NATF Form 84 (online ordering).
Bureau of Land Management
General Land Office Records (glorecords.blm.gov), a searchable index of federal land patents with images of the patents. Patents found so far for some twenty men and women of the roll and their families, several of them not yet certain matches, run from David Park’s in Carson Valley (December 1864) through the Deer Lodge Valley (from 1871), Soda Springs and Blackfoot (from 1882) and Walla Walla (1890); each is cited in the notes on the roll.
Weber State University
Oral history of Clayton Holt (OH10_046), a teacher who researched the Morrisites in South Weber.

Joseph Morris letters and revelation manuscripts

CR 1234 1 · digitized
Held by
Church History Library, Salt Lake City
Contents
Morris’s letters to Brigham Young (1857–1860), the originals of the copies in the USU collection, and manuscript revelations from 1859–1862, in Brigham Young’s office files (President’s Office Files, General Office Files: folders “Revelations of Joseph Morris, 1858–1860” and “1861–1862”). No letter from Young to Morris has been found. Kirk Watson’s edition transcribes twelve letters of 1858–1860 with item-by-item links (see Bibliography); a dated list is on the Documents page.

Church of the Firstborn (Morrisites) records, 1864–1901

CHL MS 31876 · MS 32226 · digitized
Held by
Church History Library, Salt Lake City
Extent
MS 31876: 3 boxes, 54 items, available only as digital images. MS 32226, 1869–1890: 1 box, 9 folders.
Contents
The records of the followers of George Williams, the Prophet Cainan, in Council Bluffs, Omaha and the Deer Lodge Valley. MS 31876 holds Cainan’s epistles and letters to William James, John R. Eardley and the Montana followers, written from Salt Lake City and then from England (1864–1878); record books of his writings, among them Danish translations by Emanuel Strom (1872) and a Niels Jensen record (1870–1879); copies of records from St. Ann’s Hill in Surrey, England; council minutes, 1869–1891; letters of Andrew Hendrikson, William James, George Thompson, John R. Eardley, Almerin Grow and William P. and Margaret Evans Thomas; and a photograph of the Deer Lodge church building. MS 32226 holds two minutebooks (1872–1874, and the Montana council minutes, 1881–1890) with loose minutes, 1869–1874; Danish membership records for Council Bluffs and Omaha, circa 1869–1872; a record of members of stake number 2 in Montana, 1874–1887; and two letter copy books of Cainan’s letters and instructions, circa 1866–1882. Most of the minutes and many of the letters are in Danish, with some items in Welsh. The membership record of Stake No. 2 and the Montana council minutebook for 1881–1890 (MS 32226, folders 7 and 3) were read for this guide in November 2026, and in MS 31876 the letters of 1879–1882 in part, the letters to William James of 1874–1875 and the membership slips. Among them are Williams’s own note of January 29, 1882, naming James to be put before the church after his death; an envelope to James inscribed “I leave you in charge after my decease”; Williams’s letters of 1880 and 1882 about his daughter Adelaide (in full Susanna Louisa Adelaide Williams, as he gave her address about 1870), a teacher at the Episcopal school in Salt Lake City who came to Deer Lodge; S. P. Guhl’s letter from Omaha of November 1874 disputing James’s authority; John R. Eardley’s letter from Hollister, California, of November 1875; and, among the letters to Eardley (read in part), his own letter of February 1880 planning a store in Montana George Thompson’s letter from Essex of December 1886 and his letters to William P. Thomas, 1869–83; Thompson’s letter of March 23, 1882, written for the dying Williams; Williams’s letters of 1868–1873 and 1876–78 in part; his letters to Andrew Hendrikson at Council Bluffs and Omaha, 1869–73, surveyed; and the Danish council minutes of the Race Track stake for 1882–83 the Omaha minutebook, 1869–1871; and the 1869 record book in part. The rest has not yet been read.
Access
Open to research; the library lists no known copyright. Both collections can be viewed page by page in the library’s online catalog without signing in. MS 31876 · MS 32226 New

Morrisite scepters

LDS 65-3058 · 3059 · 3060
Held by
Church History Museum collection, The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, Salt Lake City
Description
Three painted wooden staffs, each with a printed inscription mounted on the shaft. The editor saw them in the museum vault and transcribed the inscriptions. Photographs courtesy of the Church History Museum, of the three scepters together (p. 116) and of an inscription band close up (p. 117), were published with the editor’s transcription of the inscriptions in Anderson, Joseph Morris and the Saga of the Morrisites (Revisited) (2010), 116–17. They are not reproduced here.
Inscriptions
Joseph Morris’s scepter declares him sent “to RULE over all ADAM’S posterity” and is signed “Joseph Morris, PRESIDENT of the Melchisidec Priesthood.” The other two belonged to Hugh Park (“number five,” his name written over an earlier one) and Daniel Smith (“number seven”), each presenting himself as a ruler “over all Abraham’s seed.”
